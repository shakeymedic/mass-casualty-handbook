## 3. The pseudo-formula

| Claim | Verdict | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| "Death / Critical Respiratory Failure = f(Central Brainstem Depression + Muscarinic Bronchorrhea/Bronchospasm + Nicotinic Diaphragmatic Paralysis)" | Non-clinical | This is not a clinical score, equation or validated model. It has no units, no coefficients, no derivation and no source; it is a prose list dressed as mathematics. ## Safe-to-teach statements

Each statement below is supported by a source read in full in this session. Doses still require local formulary and scope-of-practice sign-off.

**Fire, smoke and burns** (all from the FPHC/BBA Pre-hospital Management of Burns Consensus Statement v2, April 2020 — https://fphc.rcsed.ac.uk/media/2957/2020-09-20-burns-consensus.pdf ; graded C/D, i.e. consensus/expert opinion):

1. Standard pulse oximeters cannot differentiate carboxyhaemoglobin from oxyhaemoglobin, so CO poisoning may be present despite a normal SpO2 reading.
2. Where a CO meter is available, a level >30% indicates severe poisoning. In confirmed CO poisoning, give 100% normobaric oxygen until COHb is normal (<3%).
3. High-flow oxygen via a non-rebreathing reservoir mask at 10–15 L/min; in the absence of CO poisoning, aim for SpO2 94–98%.
4. Prophylactic tracheal intubation (large bore) is recommended where there is suspicion of impending airway compromise — full-thickness facial burns, stridor, respiratory distress, swelling on laryngoscopy, smoke inhalation or singed nasal hairs.
5. If a surgical airway is required through burned neck tissue: a **longitudinal** skin incision to expose the anatomy, **followed by a transverse incision** into the identified cricothyroid membrane.
6. Give a cyanide antidote plus high-flow oxygen to any fire casualty with suspected smoke inhalation showing altered mental status and/or cardiovascular instability. Adult hydroxocobalamin dose **5 g IV over 15 min** (Cyanokit SmPC: second 5 g may follow; maximum total 10 g). Paediatric: **70 mg/kg, not exceeding 5 g** (max total 140 mg/kg, not exceeding 10 g) — https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4786/smpc
7. Initiate fluid resuscitation for adult **and** paediatric burns >20% TBSA; weight-based. For children, use a crystalloid with higher sodium content (0.9% sodium chloride) if available. Warm the fluid.
8. Estimate burn severity in the bands <20%, 20–50% and >50% TBSA, using a Lund & Browder chart or electronic equivalent.
9. Cool running water for 20 minutes is strongly recommended, ideally within 10 minutes and up to 3 hours from injury; water <20°C (optimum ~12°C); ice water is not recommended.
10. Hydrogel dressings are not supported by the evidence and are not superior to water; consider them only in a resource-limited setting when running water is unavailable.
11. Avoid hypothermia through active warming measures and regular temperature monitoring.
12. Revised Baux score = age + %TBSA + 17 (if inhalation injury), derived and validated as a predictor of **probability of death** — https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0b013e3181c453b3
13. WHO EMT burns MCI structures: a **Burn Rapid Response Team (BRRT)** is a small senior team deploying in 6–12 h to support **first receiving hospitals** (definitive triage, assessment, patient distribution) and is **not** expected to work on scene; a **Burn Specialist Team (BST)** arrives 48–72 h or later and remains for up to about 6 weeks for sustained clinical care — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.burns.2020.07.001

**Nerve agents:**

14. Salisbury, 4 March 2018: Sergei and Yulia Skripal, plus three further critically ill patients, were cared for in Salisbury District Hospital's ICU; pre-COVID this was the longest-running major incident in NHS history — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bja.2021.10.008
15. Dawn Sturgess was exposed on 30 June 2018 in Amesbury and died on 8 July 2018; she was the only fatality of the five people poisoned — https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2025/dawn-sturgess-inquiry-final-report-published/
16. Novichok (A-series) agents inhibit acetylcholinesterase, producing muscarinic, nicotinic and CNS toxicity; AChE inhibited by A-agents ages rapidly (ageing half-time of A-230 comparable to soman, 2–4 min), making oxime reactivation difficult — https://doi.org/10.1007/s00204-022-03437-5
17. Current therapy comprises atropine, oximes and benzodiazepines, with supportive care including mechanical ventilation — https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2501266
18. UK ED guidance for suspected Novichok poisoning (PHE/NHSE, July 2018): atropine IV/IM/IO, **4.0–4.2 mg** in adults and children ≥12 years (50–75 microgram/kg under 12), **repeated every 5 minutes until secretions are minimal and the patient is atropinised** (lungs clear, HR >80/min, adequate BP). **Pupil size must not be used as an endpoint.** The most important differential is opiate overdose, and a trial of naloxone should be given initially — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/738497/ED_briefing_note_nerve_agents.pdf
19. Primary decontamination = remove and double-bag clothing; wash skin with soap/detergent and water where time allows — same source as 18.

**Explicitly not safe to teach from this report:** hydroxocobalamin 100 mg/kg; atropine "1.5–10 mg" or "5–10 mg" starting doses; a 20 mg atropine ceiling; magnesium sulfate 4–6 g of 20% over 24 h; intravenous lipid emulsion; the "Death / Critical Respiratory Failure = f(...)" formula; "4 critical ICU admissions" and "6 investigated exposures"; BRRT/BST as UK structures; revised Baux as a validated predictor of ICU requirement.

**Not checked:** JRCALC (UK Ambulance Services Clinical Practice Guidelines) and TOXBASE are subscription resources and were not retrievable in this session. Every paramedic-tier drug dose in lines 724–777 must be reconciled against the current JRCALC monograph and the relevant trust CBRN SOP before use.
